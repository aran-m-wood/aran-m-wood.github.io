What presence, if any, does White-Collar Delinquency have in the Crypto Industry?

Mar 2023 | Aaron M. Wood

Master of Advanced Study thesis, Criminology, Law and Society, University of California, Irvine. Chair: Professor Bryan Sykes. Submitted under the name Aaron M. Hermosillo.

Abstract

This paper examines the presence of white-collar delinquency in the crypto industry. It examines the numerous sorts of white-collar crime performed in the cryptocurrency industry, and the motives behind these crimes. It also investigates the possibility of increased white-collar delinquency in the crypto industry, as well as the repercussions for the industry. Finally, it looks at the potential for law enforcement to combat white-collar delinquency in the crypto industry. The paper concludes that white-collar delinquency is a rising issue in the cryptocurrency business, and that law enforcement must take action to confront it.

Theory

The convenience theory, deterrence theory, game theory, and incentives are valuable tools for understanding white-collar crime in the crypto industry. The convenience theory suggests that a lack of regulation and oversight creates an environment where criminal activity is most likely to occur. Deterrence theory proposes that individuals are less likely to engage in criminal behavior if they fear punishment or retribution. Game theory suggests that potential rewards can outweigh the risks, encouraging individuals to engage in criminal activities. Finally, incentives can effectively encourage people to report suspicious activity or provide information about criminal networks involved in white-collar crime.

Conclusion

In summary, the crypto sector, like any other financial system, may be utilized for good and evil. It would be wrong to assume that the crypto business as a whole is a haven for white-collar criminals, although such activity has been documented. Many in the sector are engaged in proactive efforts to curb fraud, and new rules and guidelines are being implemented to increase openness and limit misconduct. As with financial fraud and insider trading in more established financial markets, white-collar crime in the cryptocurrency industry might be considered a continuation of similar practices from the past. However, some white-collar crime may find new openings in the crypto business due to its decentralized nature, low visibility, and high anonymity.

Policy memo: five recommendations

  1. Clear regulatory rules and penalties. Lack of governmental control in the cryptocurrency market has allowed scammers and fraudsters to exploit vulnerable investors. Therefore, we urge establishing clear regulatory rules and a framework for imposing legal penalties against individuals who engage in fraudulent activity.
  2. Education and forced disclosures. It is vital to promote education and awareness among investors and the general public regarding the hazards of participating in cryptocurrency. This can be achieved through various methods, including public campaigns, and forced disclosures by exchanges and other crypto-related organizations.
  3. Collaboration with law enforcement. The crypto business has the potential to collaborate closely with law enforcement organizations to detect and prosecute fraudulent operations. Therefore, there should be increased communication and collaboration between law enforcement agencies and the crypto business to ensure that individuals who engage in fraudulent activities face legal consequences.
  4. Enhance the security and transparency of exchanges. The lack of security and transparency in cryptocurrency exchanges has contributed to many fraud and theft incidents. Therefore, we urge that mandatory audits and third-party oversight be implemented to improve the security and transparency of cryptocurrency exchanges.
  5. Establish a nationwide database of fraud. A nationwide database of fraudulent actions in the cryptocurrency business can be created to assist law enforcement authorities, investors, and the public identify frauds. This database should be periodically maintained and updated.
